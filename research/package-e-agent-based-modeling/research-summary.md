# Evidence Package E
# Agent-Based Modeling and Simulation of Complex Systems

## Purpose

This evidence package explains why agent-based modeling (ABM) is an appropriate research methodology for studying complex social systems such as hiring. It provides the methodological foundation for the capstone by explaining why simulations are commonly used to investigate systems where controlled real-world experiments are impractical, expensive, or unethical.

Unlike previous packages, this package does not focus on hiring or personality. Instead, it explains why computational simulation is an appropriate way to explore how repeated decision rules can produce emergent organizational outcomes.

# Executive Summary

Agent-based modeling (ABM) is a computational research method used to study complex systems composed of many interacting individuals, or "agents." Rather than predicting the behavior of specific people or organizations, ABMs explore how simple decision rules, repeated across many interactions, can produce large-scale system behavior known as emergent phenomena.

ABMs have been widely applied across economics, sociology, epidemiology, ecology, organizational science, and public policy because many important systems cannot be studied through controlled experiments. Researchers often use simulation when manipulating real-world systems would be impractical, unethical, or prohibitively expensive.

This methodology aligns closely with the goals of this capstone. The simulation does not attempt to reproduce any specific company's hiring algorithm. Instead, it models synthetic candidates interacting with configurable hiring rules over repeated hiring rounds to examine whether different personality-weighting strategies produce different workforce outcomes.

Gilbert and Troitzsch describe ABM as particularly valuable when researchers seek to understand how individual decisions give rise to collective behavior. Similarly, Epstein argues that computational models serve as "generative" explanations by showing how observed patterns could arise from simple local interactions rather than claiming that they necessarily do.

This evidence package therefore supports the methodological legitimacy of using an agent-based simulation to investigate one plausible mechanism through which repeated hiring decisions may influence workforce composition over time.

# Key Takeaways

- Agent-based modeling is an established research methodology.
- ABMs study how individual behaviors generate system-level outcomes.
- Simulations are useful when controlled experiments are impractical or unethical.
- ABMs generate plausible mechanisms rather than exact predictions.
- Emergent behavior is a defining characteristic of many complex systems.
- The capstone uses ABM to explore one possible hiring mechanism rather than replicate a commercial hiring platform.

# Current Research Consensus

## Strong Evidence

- Agent-based modeling is widely used to study complex adaptive systems.
- ABMs are appropriate for studying emergence.
- Simulations allow researchers to test hypothetical scenarios safely.
- Individual decision rules can produce unexpected collective outcomes.
- Validation focuses on whether the model reasonably represents the mechanism being studied rather than perfectly reproducing reality.

## Mixed / Ongoing Debate

- How much realism an ABM requires.
- The best methods for validating simulations.
- The balance between model simplicity and real-world complexity.
- Generalizability of simulation findings.
- Appropriate interpretation of simulation results.

**Key Findings**

- Computational models generate plausible mechanisms.

# Living Claim Map

## Supported Claims

- Agent-based modeling is an accepted research methodology.
- Simulations are appropriate for studying complex adaptive systems.
- Emergent outcomes can arise from repeated local interactions.
- Synthetic agents are commonly used to study hypothetical scenarios.
- ABMs are useful for comparing alternative decision rules.

## Mixed Claims

- Simulations accurately predict real organizations.
- More complex simulations are always better.
- Simple models cannot provide meaningful insight.
- ABMs eliminate uncertainty.

## Claims the Literature Cannot Make

- Every simulation accurately represents reality.
- One simulation can validate organizational theory.
- ABMs replace empirical research.

## Claims My Simulation Cannot Make

- The simulation cannot predict actual hiring outcomes.
- The simulation cannot estimate real workforce demographics.
- The simulation cannot validate commercial hiring algorithms.
- The simulation represents one plausible mechanism rather than the complete hiring process.

The defensible conclusion is:

Agent-based modeling provides an appropriate methodology for exploring how repeated personality-based decision rules may generate emergent workforce patterns over time without claiming to reproduce real organizational behavior.
